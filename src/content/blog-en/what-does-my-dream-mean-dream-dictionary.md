You wake up and one image stays with you. A staircase. An animal. A house you do not know. So you type it into Google, land in a dream dictionary, read three sentences about "staircase", and think: hm.

Maybe some of it even fits. But the dream as a whole stays silent. And the question that got you looking things up at six in the morning is still open: **what am I supposed to do with this?**

That is exactly how it was for me for years. I started following my own dreams in 2011, and my first method was the obvious one: look it up. Only when I learned the Aisling Method did I understand why looking things up is never enough on its own.

One thing I will say straight away, because otherwise this would not be honest: **I work with a symbol book myself.** The Aisling Method has over 3,000 entries, grown out of three decades of practice. So the difference is not whether a symbol book is involved. It is what I do with it afterwards.

Here are the five reasons.

## 1. A dictionary knows things, your dream tells movements

Open any dream dictionary and you find nouns: snake, water, teeth, house, car. Things.

But very few dreams are made of things. They are made of **movements, directions and decisions**. You walk away. You go down. You try to get past something. You want to be alone. You do not go into the centre.

In Aisling work those are the load-bearing elements. "Wanting to be alone", for example, stands for avoidance: what you avoid in a dream is often exactly what it is about. And if you walk down a staircase, that can mean you are moving from the head into the heart.

A dictionary that only knows objects skips half your dream. Usually the more important half.

## 2. The general meaning is rarely your meaning

I once had a dream where I was sitting in the back seat of a car with Nena.

A dictionary would have said: the back seat means you are not steering your own life. Take the wheel.

That sounded plausible. It was not the message. When I took time with the dream, it became clear that Nena, a singer, a woman who performs and uses her voice, stood for my own gift. The statement was not "take control of your life". It was: **stop putting your gift in the back seat.**

That is a different sentence. It led to a different action.

If I had stopped at the dictionary definition, I would never have heard it. And that is not even the biggest problem: different dream dictionaries often give the same symbol different meanings. Which one do you follow then?

## 3. Many dream images are in no dictionary at all

Dreams speak in images built for you. Not for everyone.

Old, broken, overgrown stone steps. An animal that comes part of the way towards you, stops and waits. A voice you can hear while the person stays invisible.

Look those up. You will find nothing.

And that is not the exception. In most of the dreams people send me, the most important images are precisely the ones that appear in no dictionary. Not because they mean nothing, but because they are cut too exactly to one person.

## 4. A dictionary joins nothing together

Say you decode three symbols correctly. What do you have then?

Three fragments. And the whole rest of the dream still unexplained. But a dream is not a bag of symbols. It is a story, with an order, with a mood, with a beginning and a point where it breaks off.

A dream that plays out in sunshine says something different from the same dream in fog. An animal that attacks says something different from an animal that waits and only reacts when you avoid it. The order carries the meaning, and order cannot be looked up.

## 5. The real question is not "what", but "why now"

This is where dictionaries stop.

Suppose your dream shows you a hardness you carry in yourself. A dictionary might tell you: that stands for severity. Fine. And then?

I keep asking: why is this dream coming now? What does it have to do with the situation you are actually in? And what is it inviting you to do?

One more thing that matters to me. In this work every figure in your dream is a part of you. The unpleasant ones too. A dream never tells you what someone else should change, it speaks about your own part in it. That sounds strange at first, but it is the freeing part: if everything in your dream is you, then everything that can be changed sits with you as well.

## What works instead

No secret, just craft.

**Symbol knowledge as a beginning, not an answer.** I know what the tradition sees in an image. That is the first step, not the last.

**Your life around it.** The same dream means something different for someone who is moving house than for someone waiting on a diagnosis. That is why I always ask about your situation.

**The structure of the dream.** What comes first, what comes after, where it breaks off. A dream that ends at the exact moment of contact is already saying something by doing that.

**And then the listening.** I take time with your dream until the whole thing comes together. That is the part I cannot put into rules, and the part without which the rules are not enough.

Grounded and intuitive at once. Both, without it floating off.

## Common questions

**So are dream dictionaries useless?**
No. They are a good way in, to get a feel for symbolic language at all. They just cannot resolve your dream, because they do not know your situation and do not put the story together.

**Why do different people get different interpretations of the same symbol?**
Because the symbol is only the vocabulary, not the sentence. Water for someone going through a separation reads differently from water for someone just starting a course of training.

**Do I have to remember my dream completely?**
No. Fragments are often enough. More important than completeness is noting the feeling you woke up with.

**What do you need from me for an interpretation?**
The dream, as closely as you remember it, and a few sentences about where you are in your life right now. Nothing more.

## To close

Your dreams are not random images. They say something fairly precise about where you are standing, and they do it every night, whether you listen or not.

A dictionary gets you part of the way. It gives you vocabulary. It just stops there, and the part where vocabulary becomes a message is only beginning at that point.

The most common message I get starts like this: "I'm sure I dream a lot, but I can never remember." The first part is almost always true. The second part can be changed, faster than most people think.

Remembering is not a talent. It is a habit. And the habit that sets it off is a dream journal.

## Why the memory disappears so fast

A dream sits in short-term memory. It is not built to survive the day. If the first thing you do on waking is look at your phone, think about the nine o'clock meeting, or get up and make coffee, it is gone in seconds. Not because it was unimportant, but because your brain is doing exactly what it is meant to do: tidying up.

The good news is that your brain learns quickly what matters to you. If you write something down every morning for two weeks, it starts keeping dreams for you. People who "never" dreamed before suddenly remember two or three a night.

## The method, cut back to the essentials

**Put something beside your bed.** A notebook and a pen. Not your phone. The light and the notifications pull you straight out of the half-sleep where the memory still sits.

**Do not move straight away.** Stay lying down for two minutes after waking, in exactly the position you woke in. Memory clings to body position surprisingly strongly.

**Write fragments, not a story.** "Blue house. My sister. I couldn't lock the door." That is entirely enough. Anyone waiting to produce a finished piece of writing ends up writing nothing at all.

**Write in the present tense.** "I am standing in front of a blue house" brings you back closer to the experience than "I was standing in front of a blue house".

**Note the feeling.** This is the part almost everyone leaves out, and the part that carries the most weight in an interpretation. Was it oppressive, calm, urgent, sad? A dream where you fly and feel free says something different from one where you fly and are afraid.

**Give the dream a title.** A single line: "The door that will not shut." Titles force you to name the core, and they are how you find old entries again.

## What you see after a few weeks

The real gain is not in any single dream but in the pile. After four or five weeks you will notice things that are invisible in one night:

- **Repetitions.** The same house, the same person, the same situation. What repeats is usually not finished yet.
- **Series.** Dreams often come in runs, working through one theme across several nights.
- **Timing.** A dream from the evening before a difficult decision reads very differently once you know the decision was coming.

That is exactly why I always ask about your situation when I interpret. A symbol has no fixed meaning you could simply look up. Water is not "always the subconscious". Water can mean something different for someone who is moving house than for someone waiting on a diagnosis.

## One common mistake

Many people start out wanting to interpret their dreams as they go. They look up every symbol and write the meaning beside it. That is understandable and usually gets in the way. What you are then holding on to is no longer the dream, but your first guess about the dream. And the first guess is almost always the most obvious one, not the right one.

Write down only what happened first. The interpretation can wait. The dream cannot.

## When you get stuck

Some dreams will not resolve, however often you read them. Usually they are precisely the ones that stay longest: the recurring one, the one that was too intense, the one you woke from knowing it mattered.
